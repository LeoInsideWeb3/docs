1 · Overview & Decisions

Moving All Perpetuals to A-Book

The platform exits the counterparty role for perpetuals: the order book, klines, and 24h statistics come straight from Binance; every user order is genuinely executed on Binance via the market maker (MM); the platform keeps accounts, margin, liquidation, and the fee system, earning explicit fees instead of carrying internal matching and counterparty market risk.

Market data & mappingBinance-firstExecution / market data / klines all keyed to Binance; maintain only our ↔ Binance symbol mapping

Three Preconditions Worth Stating Up Front

Precondition 1: zero open positions platform-wide at the switch instant (confirmed — fresh start)

At the switch instant there must be no open positions platform-wide — legacy positions would have no Binance hedge, splitting books from reality.

Internal-account cleanup in progress: attacker executed (09-22), 0x535c / 0x7152 runbooks ready. No user positions need disposal (halted state treated as a fresh start, confirmed 2026-09-24).

Precondition 2: perp_trigger and the liquidation scanner stay in place

Conditional-order trigger evaluation, WAL, and LockPerpAsset are all local (the perp_trigger Trigger→Lock→CommandOrderExecute flow is unchanged).

The PM Perp liquidation scanner (mark-driven, MMR tiers) is unchanged — only the close orders' receiver changes from Finex to mm-connector. Zero change to user semantics.

Precondition 3: keep a B-book rollback switch for one release cycle

The finex-perp engine is routed off but not deleted.

For one release cycle after cutover we keep a hot "route flag flips back to Finex" capability (page 9); cleanup happens after the observation window once A-Book is stable.

2 · Architecture

Today → Target: Replace the Matching Engine, Keep Every Contract

The core insight: Finex exposes exactly two Kafka contracts — ingress commands.{MKT}.OrderCommands, egress events.{MKT}.EventOrderUpdated + events.{MKT}.EventTradeExecuted. Swap the role that consumes the ingress and produces the egress to mm-connector, and every downstream service needs zero changes.

Today (B-Book internal matching)

trading-apiedge gateway

① gRPC LockPerpAsset locks margin (reject on failure) → ② publishes CommandOrderCreate to commands.{MKT}.OrderCommands; publish failure rolls the lock back (order_operation_core.go:328-351).

↓ Kafka
Finex perpin-memory matching · 3 shards

Price-time priority matches user orders against each other; emits order book deltas (orderbook.{MKT}) plus trade/order events. Users are each other's counterparties.

↓ EventTradeExecuted (maker + taker both internal accounts)
PM Perptwo-participant settlement

Settlement.HandleTrade updates positions, realized PnL, fees, and margin for both sides; on liquidation the insurance/settlement system accounts (owner=0x0) take the counterparty leg (CLEARING_MIRROR), with takeover/ADL handling bad debt.

↓ trade events
ledger / analyticsdata side

ledger aggregates internal trades → Timescale klines/tickers; one mark-oracle source is InternalPerpTradeCollector (internal trades as self-price).

Target (A-Book via MM execution)

trading-apiunchanged

Order placement / margin locking / rollback paths completely unchanged. Only the data source for order book, klines, ticker, and the public trade tape switches to quote's Binance mirror (same topics, same formats).

↓ Kafka commands.{MKT}.OrderCommands (same topic, different consumer)
mm-connectornew · takes Finex's seat

Consumes order commands → submits via MM REST (idempotent + expires_at) → WS reports drive the state machine → translates ack/fill/cancel/reject into EventOrderUpdated / EventTradeExecuted (house synthetic order as counterparty leg) → publishes to events.{MKT}.*. Built in: dangling-order arbitration, timeout auto-reject, heartbeat circuit breaker, three-way reconciliation worker.

↓ REST / WS (page 3 contract) · MM → Binance execution
PM Perpsettlement semantics unchanged + house account

Still consumes EventTradeExecuted for two-participant settlement — except the counterparty leg is now always the MM house system account (UUID suffix 09, owner=0x0). Liquidation close orders go to the connector; takeover/ADL retire; the insurance fund only covers bankruptcy shortfalls.

↓ Binance public streams (quote mirror, page 6)
quote + ledgerdata plane switch

quote subscribes to Binance depth/kline/ticker/aggTrade and publishes in existing formats; ledger's perp-market kline source switches from "internal trade aggregation" to "Binance kline direct write" (per-market flag); InternalPerpTradeCollector is disabled for A-Book markets.

Why the Kafka-native Option Was Rejected

The alternative was letting the MM consume/produce our Kafka topics directly (no connector needed). Rejected because:

  1. Security boundary — the Kafka cluster network and credentials would be exposed to an external team; consumer-group, rebalance, and lag management responsibility shifts outward with an uncontrolled blast radius.
  2. Coupled evolution — internal event schema changes (e.g. adding a field to EventTradeExecuted) would directly break external consumers; a REST/WS contract lets internal formats evolve independently.
  3. MM implementation cost — the MM team knows Binance-style REST/WS. The interface mirrors Binance semantics (order placement / cancels / execution stream), so their implementation is near zero learning cost.
  4. Governance — the connector is a first-class service on our side: rate limiting, circuit breaking, reconciliation, and audit logs all live with us — observable and operable.

  "transact_time":   "2026-10-01T08:00:00.871Z"
}

Why role matters

Our lock side pre-charges fees by order type (post_only locks at the maker rate); at settlement we charge by the Binance fill role the MM reports: a resting limit that gets hit → role=maker → maker rate; a crossing order → taker rate. Fully aligned with Finex's maker/taker semantics today — fee_engine needs no changes.

3.5 Idempotency · Dangling Orders · Arbitration

  1. Every submit carries expires_at (our clock + 2 × RTT + processing headroom, default 3s)A request already expired on arrival must be answered with EXPIRED and must not be executed — the first gate against the fatal window of "we timed out, declared the order dead, and unlocked margin — while the MM fills it in flight".
  2. ack timeout (500ms) → switch to GET arbitrationThe connector calls GET /api/v1/orders/{client_order_id} for the timed-out order: no MM record → locally REJECTED + unlock + reject event; record in-flight → keep tracking; record filled → accept all fills through normal settlement (lock converts to settlement).
  3. WS disconnect → full arbitration after reconnectFirst step on reconnect: GET every in-flight client_order_id, aligning states and fill sequences; fill_id dedup guarantees no double settlement.
  4. Double idempotency insurance on settlementThe connector's synthesized events carry IdempotencyKey = client_order_id:fill_id; PM Perp's existing idempotency cache dedups natively (reusing Settlement.HandleTrade's Set/Delete logic).

5 · Settlement & Accounting

The MM House Account: the Last Piece of Double-Entry

Under the Principal model, the MM's hedge PnL happens on Binance (his money) — but our books must record where every payment obligation to users comes from. The solution: one new system account — the MM house account — as the counterparty leg of every trade, preserving Scheme A double-entry and the audit chain by construction.

House Account Definition

Reuses the existing system-account machinery exactly (the spec list in raft_recovery/system_accounts.go + EnsureSystemAccounts idempotent reconciliation; UUID suffix continues at 09):

// portfolio_manager_perp/perpetuals/domain/models/const.go addition:
MMHouseAccountAppSessionID = "00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000009"
MMHouseAccountID          = "00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000009"
// owner = SystemAccountOwner (0x0), PrincipalKindService, auto-provisioned via EnsureSystemAccounts
  • Semantics — balance = our net debt to the MM (positive = we owe the MM; negative = the MM owes us). Zeroed by cash settlement each cycle; the balance folds into commercial settlement (terms pending).
  • Fees — the house leg pays zero platform fees (the MM's compensation rides the commercial split, not the trading-fee ledger).
  • Risk exemption — excluded from liquidation scanning, no margin requirement (same treatment as insurance/settlement pools).

The economic loop (a user long, start to finish)

User opens long 10 TAO @199 → we lock IMR → MM buys the 10 TAO hedge on Binance.

User closes the long @205 → synthesized-event settlement: user +60 (realized PnL credited), house −60 (our net debt to the MM +60); the MM's Binance hedge earned +60, paid to him at the next cycle settlement.

At any instant: house net exposure + user long/short netting = 0. When two users are natural counterparties (A buys, B sells) the two house legs cancel and the MM needs no Binance hedge order — spread and fees still collected.

Funding Pass-Through

  • Rate & interval — Binance values used directly: the mark oracle's funding field already has a Binance source; perps_markets.funding_interval_seconds already supports variable intervals (the rollout seeded 4h/8h per Binance fundingInfo). PM Perp's funding worker and funding_payments settlement logic unchanged.
  • Accounting path — user funding paid/received books against the house account (no need to charge a user counterparty — the counterparty is the house); the MM's Binance hedge funding is same notional, same timestamps, opposite direction, netting naturally; residuals (user position-change timing gaps) accrue to the house balance and settle per cycle.
  • Cap / interest — in pass-through mode the cap always equals the actual Binance value; our self-set caps become unnecessary (current table values already mirror Binance, no change needed).

Liquidation & the Insurance Fund

  1. Scanner unchanged — stage1 cross/isolated executors' trigger evaluation, MMR tiers, and warning bands (80/90/95) all retained.
  2. Close outlet changes — no more liquidation IOCs into Finex; instead a source=liquidation market close order goes to the connector, the MM unwinds on Binance, and the reported fills settle (ExecType=liquidation; Scheme A journal_id grouping reused).
  3. Bankruptcy handling — the part of a user's loss exceeding their margin: insurance fund → house account transfer (the existing CLEARING_MIRROR SettleLiquidatedPosition semantics retained, simplified from "settlement pool takes over the position" to "pay the difference directly").
  4. Retired — takeover (insurance fund inheriting positions) and ADL (auto-deleveraging counterparties) go offline: A-Book has no internal counterparties to protect. The insurance fund's sole remaining duty is covering bankruptcy shortfalls.

Implementation notes for the depth mirror

Binance depth20 is a snapshot stream (no diff-alignment problem) — full 20 levels every 100ms → maps directly onto the existing OrderBookSnapshot semantics; increments are synthesized by quote as snapshot diffs, preserving the current client mix of increment/snapshot consumption.

Note: the mirrored book contains only Binance's orders — our users' resting orders are not on it. A user's own order state arrives via the private stream (EventOrderUpdated); the frontend overlays "my orders" on the mirrored book (frontend change item, tracked in the cutover plan).

ledger: Kline Source Switch

  1. New ingestion path — quote publishes events.{MKT}.EventKlineObserved (1m base); ledger consumes and writes directly to Timescale OHLC and the 24h ticker aggregates instead of computing from internal trades.
  2. Per-market flag — ledger config marks the data source per market: A-Book markets = Binance klines; spot and (transition-period) non-A-Book markets = internal trade aggregation (existing logic).
  3. Higher-timeframe aggregation — continuous aggregates above the 1m base (5m/1h/…) keep using the existing Timescale materialized views.
  4. History continuity — klines aggregated from internal trades are retained; the switch date is the boundary; frontend charts stitch across it (continuous time axis, no rewrite).
  5. User trade history — unaffected: user trade records have always lived in PM Perp (perps_transactions / position history), independent of public klines.

Mark Price & Risk Data

  • Multi-source oracle retained — Binance-dominant weights, OKX/Bybit as backup; the min_sources = 1 rule never changes (the rollout runbook's permanent-lock clause stays in force).
  • InternalPerpTradeCollector disabled — the "internal trade self-price" source goes offline for A-Book markets (user fills are sparse and priced at Binance anyway — no information content).
  • Liquidation reference price — naturally aligned with Binance's liquidation behavior (both use Binance mark), eliminating the mismatch of "our liquidation price ≠ hedge-market liquidation price" — a hidden benefit of A-Book.
  • exchangeInfo changes — when Binance tightens filters, quote syncs within the hour; resting orders that become invalid are cleaned up naturally by MM-side INVALID_FILTER reports.

The spot Boundary: Shared Pipelines & the Isolation Principle

Much of quote / ledger / trading-api is shared code between spot and perps. Every A-Book change is routed by market type (the -PERP suffix gate / split market lists); global switches are forbidden — that is the guarantee of zero spot impact.

The boundary of suspended liquidation

While suspended, user margin ratios keep deteriorating (mark never stops); recovery closes at worse prices — the insurance-fund share grows, but no orphaned positions can appear (our books stay self-consistent).

That is the cost of the "degrade, never lose funds" principle; it must be included in insurance-fund capacity modeling (open items).

Cutover Verification Checklist

  • Order book mirror — frontend depth matches Binance for the same symbol; increment/snapshot mixed stream without jumps; latency < 300ms.
  • Full order path — resting limit + fill, market with multiple fills, post_only rejection, FOK full rejection, IOC remainder expiry — at least one of each of the five.
  • Cancels and arbitration — normal cancel, cancel race (already filled), an intentionally induced POST timeout walking the GET arbitration path.
  • Settlement correctness — spot-check fills for position / PnL / fees (maker/taker rates by role); house balance matches a manual walkthrough.
  • Kline continuity — 1m klines stitch across the switch date without gaps; higher timeframes produce values normally.
  • Liquidation drill — a controlled account pushed to its MMR trigger: close order executed via the MM, ExecType=liquidation, bankruptcy shortfall lands in house, Scheme A journal complete.
  • Circuit-breaker drill — cut the MM sandbox: 15s break takes effect, new orders rejected, cancel-all issued, reconnect arbitration recovers.
  • Reconciliation clean — first rounds of both jobs (positions / fills) all green.
  • spot regression (dedicated) — spot kline continuity spot checks; spot order book and public trade tape baselines compared before and after cutover (verifies market-type gating on shared pipelines).

Rollback (valid for one release cycle)

# Trigger: an A-Book systemic problem that cannot be fixed quickly (persistent reconciliation drift / MM long-term unavailable)
① trading-api halts new orders + cancel-all
② drain in-flight orders (if positions exist: record and decide hedging manually — rollback wants positions at zero)
③ route flag flips back to finex-perp: connector stops consuming OrderCommands, finex-perp shards restart and load markets
④ quote market data flips back (order book producer returns to finex; klines resume internal aggregation —
   the Binance klines already written during the window are kept)
⑤ verify → reopen

# After rollback, A-Book history already written is untouched (trade history / house balance / audit)

The hard constraint of rollback

If positions opened during the A-Book window exist at rollback time, Finex's internal matching after switching back has no counterparty liquidity — within the rollback window, treat open positions as exceptions requiring manual handling. That is why the window is one release cycle: the longer it drags on, the less realistic rollback becomes — which is what the observation window is for, surfacing problems early.
