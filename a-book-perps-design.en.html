1 · Overview & Decisions

Moving All Perpetuals to A-Book

The platform exits the counterparty role for perpetuals: the order book, klines, and 24h statistics come straight from Binance; every user order is genuinely executed on the platform's own Binance master account through our in-house market maker (MM); the platform keeps accounts, margin, liquidation pricing, and the fee system, earning explicit fees instead of carrying internal matching and counterparty market risk. Since v1.4 every A-Book position is isolated and one-way: we compute each position's liquidation price, the MM holds a matching stop order on Binance, and that stop's real fill is the user's liquidation.

Three Preconditions Worth Stating Up Front

Precondition 1: zero open positions platform-wide at the switch instant (confirmed — fresh start)

At the switch instant there must be no open positions platform-wide — legacy positions would have no Binance hedge, splitting books from reality.

Internal-account cleanup in progress: attacker executed (09-22), 0x535c / 0x7152 runbooks ready. No user positions need disposal (halted state treated as a fresh start, confirmed 2026-09-24).

Precondition 2: user conditional orders stay local; liquidation moves to Binance-resident stops

User TP/SL trigger evaluation, WAL, and LockPerpAsset stay local (the perp_trigger Trigger→Lock→CommandOrderExecute flow is unchanged).

Liquidation changes shape (v1.4): PM Perp keeps computing each position's isolated liquidation price (MMR tiers), but no longer fires close orders itself — it publishes the price as a protection target, and the MM's stop on Binance executes the close. The scanner stays on as a shadow monitor (alerts only), so the same position can never be closed twice.

Precondition 4: isolated + one-way are enforced, not defaulted

A-Book markets reject cross-margin and hedge-mode settings server-side (PM Perp + trading-api validation); the frontend hides the Cross/Hedge toggles. One user × one market holds at most one position, so it maps to exactly one liquidation price and one stop.

Precondition 3: keep a B-book rollback switch for one release cycle

The finex-perp engine is routed off but not deleted.

For one release cycle after cutover we keep a hot "route flag flips back to Finex" capability (page 9); cleanup happens after the observation window once A-Book is stable.

2 · Architecture

Today → Target: Replace the Matching Engine, Keep Every Contract

The core insight: Finex exposes exactly two Kafka contracts — ingress commands.{MKT}.OrderCommands, egress events.{MKT}.EventOrderUpdated + events.{MKT}.EventTradeExecuted. Swap the role that consumes the ingress and produces the egress to mm-connector, and every downstream service needs zero changes.

Takeover/ADL retire; the insurance fund only covers bankruptcy shortfalls.

Why the Kafka-native Option Was Rejected

The alternative was letting the MM consume/produce our Kafka topics directly (no connector needed). Rejected because:

  1. Security boundary — the Kafka cluster network and credentials would be exposed to the MM's service (in-house, but a separately operated system); consumer-group, rebalance, and lag management responsibility shifts outward with an uncontrolled blast radius.
  2. Coupled evolution — internal event schema changes (e.g. adding a field to EventTradeExecuted) would directly break external consumers; a REST/WS contract lets internal formats evolve independently.
  3. MM implementation cost — the MM team knows Binance-style REST/WS. The interface mirrors Binance semantics (order placement / cancels / execution stream), so their implementation is near zero learning cost.
  4. Governance — the connector is a first-class service on our side: rate limiting, circuit breaking, reconciliation, and audit logs all live with us — observable and operable.

Why role matters

Our lock side pre-charges fees by order type (post_only locks at the maker rate); at settlement we charge by the Binance fill role the MM reports: a resting limit that gets hit → role=maker → maker rate; a crossing order → taker rate. Fully aligned with Finex's maker/taker semantics today — fee_engine needs no changes.

3.5 Idempotency · Dangling Orders · Arbitration

A race window that must be closed (review feedback 2026-09-25)

A GET at 500ms returning "not found" does not mean the order will never execute: the POST can still be in flight in the network or the MM's queue, arriving at 800ms and still unexpired (default validity 3s) — if we unlock margin at that point and the user withdraws, the later-arriving fill has no margin backing. expires_at constrains "when the MM receives it", not "when we may declare it dead".

  1. Every submit carries expires_at (our clock + 2 × RTT + processing headroom, default 3s)A request already expired on arrival must be answered with EXPIRED and must not be executed — the validity window is the time precondition of the death verdict, not a sufficient condition (see item 2).
  2. ack timeout (500ms) → the order enters UNKNOWN; margin stays lockedA timeout means "no ack received", not "the MM never received it". A death verdict requires all three conditions: ① expires_at + clock-skew headroom (default +1s) has passed; ② a GET at that point still finds no record; ③ the MM provides a non-executable terminal guarantee for that client_order_id (tombstone, item 3). Any "not found" before expiry is treated as UNKNOWN with margin locked, shown to the user as pending.
  3. MM-side tombstone guarantee (contract addition)The first time the MM answers a GET with "no record" for a client_order_id it must persist that answer; any later POST with the same ID is rejected. This upgrades "not found now" into "will never execute" — without it, the death verdict does not stand.
  4. The MM's own UNKNOWN obligation (contract addition)When the MM is uncertain about its Binance submission result (MM→Binance timeout), it must not report FILLED / REJECTED — it must keep the order in-flight until certain. The same problem is pushed down one level; the MM is not allowed to close its own window with a guess.
  5. CANCEL_REJECTED(FILLED) must not be ignoredWe may not yet have received all fill reports locally (WS gap): first GET to backfill the fill sequence, settle each fill, and only then may the order go terminal.
  6. WS disconnect → full arbitration after reconnectFirst step on reconnect: GET every in-flight and UNKNOWN client_order_id, aligning states and fill sequences; fill_id dedup guarantees no double settlement.
  7. Double idempotency insurance on settlementThe connector's synthesized events carry IdempotencyKey = client_order_id:fill_id; PM Perp's existing idempotency cache dedups natively (reusing Settlement.HandleTrade's Set/Delete logic).

3.8 Liquidation Protection Contract (v1.4)

Division of labour decided 2026-09-28: we compute the liquidation price, the MM keeps a Binance stop at that price. We publish a desired state per position; the MM converges Binance to it. We never place, replace, or cancel Binance stops ourselves.

// PUT /api/v1/protections/{position_key} — position_key = {app_session_id}:{market}
{
  "position_key":  "8c1e…:TAOUSDT-PERP",
  "version":       1042,                      // monotonic per position; older versions must be ignored
  "binance_symbol": "TAOUSDT",
  "side":          "sell",                    // closing side: long → sell, short → buy
  "qty":           "19.75",                   // full position size; "0" = remove the protection
  "trigger_price": "181.35",                  // our isolated liquidation price
  "working_type":  "MARK_PRICE",              // same reference as the mark we show users
  "reason":        "fill"                     // fill|margin_change|leverage|funding|resync
}

// WS reports added to the 3.4 stream
PROTECTION_APPLIED   {position_key, version, binance_stop_id, trigger_price, qty}
PROTECTION_TRIGGERED {position_key, version, binance_stop_id, transact_time}   // sent before the fills
TRADE_FILL           {…, source:"liquidation", position_key, version}          // the close fills, 3.4 format
PROTECTION_REJECTED  {position_key, version, error_code}                       // e.g. cap reached — alert

Our obligations

  • Publish on every change — after each fill (partial fills included), margin add/remove, leverage change, funding settlement, and fee debit that moves the liquidation price. Position back to zero → publish qty=0.
  • Coalesce on our side too — at most one outstanding version per position; newer targets supersede older ones before they are sent. The MM still applies only the highest version it has seen.
  • Periodic full resync — every N minutes and after every WS reconnect, re-publish the latest version for every open position (idempotent on the MM side).
  • Freeze on trigger — on PROTECTION_TRIGGERED the position enters the liquidating state (the existing isolated liquidation lock): new orders on that user × market are rejected until the liquidation settles.

MM obligations (review items)

  • Exactly one live stop per position — replace is cancel-then-place, never place-then-cancel: two live stops can both trigger and close the position twice. The short unprotected gap is accepted.
  • Cancel result "unknown order" = possibly triggered — do not place the new stop until the fate of the old one is known (triggered → report the liquidation instead).
  • Price already through — if Binance rejects the stop because the trigger would fire immediately, close at market right away and report it as a liquidation.
  • Full-quantity completion — if a triggered stop only partially fills, keep closing the remainder at market until the protected quantity is flat; mark the last fill as final.
  • Netted-account rules — the master account nets all users: never use closePosition or symbol-wide cancel, and do not rely on reduceOnly (all three act on the account's net position, i.e. on other users).
  • Binance limits are the MM's problem, visible to us — conditional orders now go through the Algo Service (/fapi/v1/algoOrder, clientAlgoId) and Binance caps open conditional orders per symbol per account (10 at the time of writing), far below "one per user". How the MM covers every position (sub-accounts, local triggering, …) is its design choice, but a position it cannot protect must be reported via PROTECTION_REJECTED, never silently dropped.
  • Attribution — every stop and every liquidation fill carries position_key + version, so the fill settles against the right user without parsing Binance ids.

Liquidation price definition (isolated, one-way)

We reuse PM Perp's existing isolated liquidation-price calculation (the value already shown to users), with MMR from the market's risk tiers. For reference: long ≈ (entry × qty − isolated margin) / (qty × (1 − MMR)); short ≈ (entry × qty + isolated margin) / (qty × (1 + MMR)), where isolated margin already reflects margin added or removed, funding paid or received, and fees debited. The trigger sits at the maintenance level, not at bankruptcy, which leaves a buffer for slippage between trigger and fill.

The local scanner never closes A-Book positions

If both the Binance stop and our scanner could close, the same position could be closed twice — and on a netted account the second close opens a real, unowned position. On A-Book markets the scanner only watches: mark past a liquidation price with no PROTECTION_TRIGGERED within T seconds raises an alert and the admin emergency close tool (manual, source=liquidation) takes over.

Order amendment

Not supported — same as today: cancel and re-place. Emergency closes and batch closes flow through the same command path; the connector submits one by one.

5 · Settlement & Accounting

The House Account: the Last Piece of Double-Entry

Every user fill is a real trade on our own Binance master account — but our books must record where every payment obligation to users comes from. The solution: one new system account — the house account — as the counterparty leg of every trade. It is the ledger mirror of the master account, preserving Scheme A double-entry and the audit chain by construction.

House Account Definition

Reuses the existing system-account machinery (the spec list in raft_recovery/system_accounts.go + EnsureSystemAccounts idempotent reconciliation; UUID suffix continues at 09). But the house sits on the hot path of every single trade — it cannot be estimated as "just another account"; it must be benchmarked and validated as a core-settlement change (review feedback 2026-09-25):

// portfolio_manager_perp/perpetuals/domain/models/const.go addition:
MMHouseAccountAppSessionID = "00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000009"
MMHouseAccountID          = "00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000009"
// owner = SystemAccountOwner (0x0), PrincipalKindService, auto-provisioned via EnsureSystemAccounts
  • Semantics — house = the ledger mirror of our Binance master account. Its realized PnL equals the master account's trading PnL on user flow; its position per market is the negative of the users' aggregate. There is no third party to settle with: house balance is reconciled against the master account's wallet, not paid out.
  • Master account margin is a platform risk — isolated margin exists only in our books. On Binance the whole net position shares one account margin, so the master account itself can be liquidated. Its margin ratio is monitored and breakered (page 8); the Binance leverage/margin setup of the master account is an operations decision, not derived from users.
  • Hot spot & concurrency — every USDT-quoted market shares one house and every trade writes it: Raft apply throughput and the dbWriter's perps_balances hot-row upsert must be measured. Fallback: per-market house pools following the insurance-fund Pool 1/2/3 precedent (1:1 with Binance symbols, reconciliation compares per market, aggregation is a plain sum).
  • Bookkeeping form: two-way HEDGE legs — every trade atomically books the user leg and the house leg (same journal), keeping both sides' realized PnL strictly in sync — no timing skew with many users sharing the account. A net-position variant would require changing open/close classification (IsClosingOrder) semantics and breaks this sync; rejected. House legs carry zero allocated margin and zero fees.
  • Risk exemptions (explicitly implemented, not obtained by default) — negative balances allowed, no MMR, excluded from liquidation scanning; synthetic orders bypass normal order validation; PrincipalKindService excludes it from user statistics / rankings / rewards / analytics aggregation, per the insurance-fund precedent.
  • No-double-booking invariant — on liquidation, a user's loss is booked exactly once in trade settlement; the bankruptcy shortfall is expressed only via the insurance → house transfer; the two must never stack. Asserted in test/v2 integration scenarios.

The economic loop (a user long, start to finish — v1.4, own master account)

User opens long 10 TAO @199 (isolated) → we lock the isolated margin → the MM buys 10 TAO on our master account → house books the short leg against the user → PM Perp publishes the protection (stop at the liquidation price).

User closes the long @205 → user +60 (realized PnL credited), house −60; on Binance the master account sold the 10 TAO for +60. The cash backing the user's gain is already in our own account — nothing to collect from anyone.

When users lose, the direction reverses: house gains, and the master account's Binance loss on that flow equals it. At any instant house mirrors the master account's user-flow position and PnL.

When two users are natural counterparties (A buys, B sells) both orders are still forwarded to Binance as-is; they simply net inside the master account's position. We never internalize matching.

Funding Pass-Through

  • Rate & interval — Binance values used directly: the mark oracle's funding field already has a Binance source; perps_markets.funding_interval_seconds already supports variable intervals (the rollout seeded 4h/8h per Binance fundingInfo). PM Perp's funding worker and funding_payments settlement logic unchanged.
  • Accounting path — user funding paid/received books against the house account. Binance charges the master account on its net position while we charge each user on their own position; with users on both sides these differ (longs and shorts net on Binance but each pays or receives with us). The difference is house PnL, booked explicitly per funding event and reconciled against the master account's Binance funding records.
  • Liquidation prices move with funding — each funding settlement changes isolated margins, so every open position is recomputed and a new protection version published in one batch. Only positions whose liquidation price moved by more than a threshold (N ticks) are re-published, and the batch is paced to stay within Binance limits around the funding timestamp.
  • Cap / interest — in pass-through mode the cap always equals the actual Binance value; our self-set caps become unnecessary (current table values already mirror Binance, no change needed).

Liquidation & the Insurance Fund

  1. Liquidation price is the output — MMR tiers and the isolated formula are retained; the scanner no longer decides when to close, it computes and publishes the liquidation price (3.8) and keeps a shadow watch (page 4).
  2. Execution on Binance — the MM's stop closes the position at Binance's real fill price; the reported fills settle as ExecType=liquidation (Scheme A journal_id grouping reused). That fill price is the liquidation price the user sees in their history.
  3. Settlement order — realized PnL → liquidation fee → remaining isolated margin returned to the user's available balance → cancel the user's other orders on that market (page 4).
  4. Bankruptcy handling — the part of a user's loss exceeding their isolated margin: insurance fund → house account transfer (the existing CLEARING_MIRROR SettleLiquidatedPosition semantics retained, simplified from "settlement pool takes over the position" to "pay the difference directly"). Isolation caps the user's loss at that one position's margin.
  5. Retired — takeover (insurance fund inheriting positions) and ADL (auto-deleveraging counterparties) go offline: A-Book has no internal counterparties to protect. The insurance fund's sole remaining duty is covering bankruptcy shortfalls.

Implementation notes for the depth mirror (incl. diff semantics, review feedback 2026-09-25)

Binance depth20 is a snapshot stream (no diff-alignment problem) — full 20 levels every 100ms → maps directly onto the existing OrderBookSnapshot semantics; increments are synthesized by quote as snapshot diffs, with two hard rules: vanishing levels must emit a qty→0 deletion increment; after reconnect, first re-publish a full snapshot to rebuild the diff baseline (no giant diff across the outage window). The current client mix of increment/snapshot consumption is preserved.

Note: the mirrored book contains only Binance's orders — our users' resting orders are not on it. A user's own order state arrives via the private stream (EventOrderUpdated); the frontend overlays "my orders" on the mirrored book (frontend change item, tracked in the cutover plan).

ledger: External Kline Ingestion Design (not "add a consumer and write directly")

Review feedback (2026-09-25): today's ledger generates continuous aggregates from trades, and higher timeframes build on those; external 1m klines cannot slot into the existing structure merely by adding a consumer that writes directly — a new storage and aggregation design is required.

  1. New source table + UPSERT semantics — a new external-kline source table (symbol, bucket, OHLCV, event-time watermark): Binance kline events carry the cumulative volume of the running minute, so an unclosed bar is overwritten event by event within its minute; duplicates / out-of-order events are idempotently upserted by event-time watermark (a late old value never rewinds an updated bucket).
  2. Disconnect backfill — WS outage windows are backfilled via REST /fapi/v1/klines through the same upsert path, naturally idempotent; when backfill and the live stream race, the higher watermark wins.
  3. Aggregation chain rebuild — perp markets' higher-timeframe CAGGs are re-pointed from the trades source to the new source table; spot and transition-period markets keep the trades chain — two chains coexist, routed per market, not a global switch.
  4. History continuity — klines aggregated from internal trades are retained; the switch date is the boundary; frontend charts stitch across it (continuous time axis, no rewrite).
  5. User trade history — unaffected: user trade records have always lived in PM Perp (perps_transactions / position history), independent of public klines.

Mark Price & Risk Data

  • A-Book mark = Binance mark (v1.4) — liquidations now trigger on Binance against its mark price (stops use working_type=MARK_PRICE), so the mark we display and use for estimates must be that same value; otherwise a user sees "price never reached my liquidation price" and is liquidated anyway. On A-Book markets the oracle weights Binance 100%; OKX/Bybit remain only as a staleness / divergence alarm. The min_sources = 1 rule never changes (the rollout runbook's permanent-lock clause stays in force).
  • InternalPerpTradeCollector disabled — the "internal trade self-price" source goes offline for A-Book markets (user fills are sparse and priced at Binance anyway — no information content).
  • Liquidation reference price — the liquidation price we publish, the stop's trigger, and the mark shown in the UI are all the same Binance mark: one number end to end.
  • exchangeInfo changes — when Binance tightens filters, quote syncs within the hour; resting orders that become invalid are cleaned up naturally by MM-side INVALID_FILTER reports.

The boundary of a broken link

Because protections live on Binance, a NeoDAX ↔ MM outage no longer suspends liquidation: applied stops keep firing and are settled when reports resume. The real exposure is the MM ↔ Binance side — a position whose latest target was never applied, or a stop Binance could not accept.

Those gaps are what the shadow scanner, protection reconciliation, and emergency close tool exist for; their residual cost belongs in insurance-fund capacity modeling (open items).

  • Full order path — resting limit + fill, market with multiple fills, post_only rejection, FOK full rejection, IOC remainder expiry — at least one of each of the five.
  • Cancels and arbitration — normal cancel, cancel race (already filled), an intentionally induced POST timeout walking the GET arbitration path.
  • Settlement correctness — spot-check fills for position / PnL / fees (maker/taker rates by role); house balance matches a manual walkthrough.
  • Kline continuity — 1m klines stitch across the switch date without gaps; higher timeframes produce values normally.
  • Isolated / one-way enforcement — cross-margin and hedge-mode requests rejected on every A-Book market; toggles hidden in the UI.
  • Protection lifecycle — open → stop applied at the published price; partial fills coalesce into one replace; add margin / change leverage moves the stop; close to zero removes it; protection reconciliation green.
  • Liquidation drill — a controlled account's position driven to its liquidation price: the Binance stop fires, fills arrive as ExecType=liquidation at the real fill price, remaining margin returned, other orders on that market cancelled, bankruptcy shortfall (if any) lands in house, Scheme A journal complete.
  • Funding recompute — after a funding settlement, affected positions get new protection versions within the paced batch; no rate-limit errors.
  • Circuit-breaker drill — cut the MM sandbox: 15s break takes effect, new orders rejected, cancel-all issued, reconnect arbitration recovers.
  • Reconciliation clean — first rounds of all jobs (net position / protections / fills / wallet) green; master account margin ratio within band.
  • spot regression (dedicated) — spot kline continuity spot checks; spot order book and public trade tape baselines compared before and after cutover (verifies market-type gating on shared pipelines).

Rollback (valid for one release cycle)

# Trigger: an A-Book systemic problem that cannot be fixed quickly (persistent reconciliation drift / MM long-term unavailable)
① trading-api halts new orders + cancel-all
② drain in-flight orders (if positions exist: record and decide hedging manually — rollback wants positions at zero)
③ route flag flips back to finex-perp: connector stops consuming OrderCommands, finex-perp shards restart and load markets
④ quote market data flips back (order book producer returns to finex; klines resume internal aggregation —
   the Binance klines already written during the window are kept)
⑤ verify → reopen

# After rollback, A-Book history already written is untouched (trade history / house balance / audit)

The hard constraint of rollback

If positions opened during the A-Book window exist at rollback time, Finex's internal matching after switching back has no counterparty liquidity — within the rollback window, treat open positions as exceptions requiring manual handling. That is why the window is one release cycle: the longer it drags on, the less realistic rollback becomes — which is what the observation window is for, surfacing problems early.
