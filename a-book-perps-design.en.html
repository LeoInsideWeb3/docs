1 · Overview & Decisions

Adding A-Book Next to B-Book, Per Market

The platform gains a second execution model and chooses it per market. B-Book is today's system: Finex matches users against each other and against internal market-making accounts, and the platform is the counterparty. A-Book forwards every user order to the platform's own Binance master account through our in-house market maker (MM): the order book, klines, and mark price come from Binance, and the platform stops being the counterparty on that market. Accounts, margin, liquidation, and fees stay in our system for both models.

What A-Book changes — and what it does not

Changes: where fills happen (Binance instead of our Finex book) and who carries the market risk. Our master account holds the opposite of our users' net position, so user profits come from the Binance market, not from the platform. Users trade against Binance depth, and a thin internal book can no longer be pushed around (the 09-18 TAO attack) or frozen (the 07-15 ETH mark incident).

Does not change: every piece of business logic — balances, margin, positions, liquidation, fees, risk checks — stays in our system, because Binance knows only our master account. The MM is an execution channel that plays Finex's role for A-Book markets.

Trade-off: revenue on A-Book markets is fees only, and every fill pays Binance fees. Hence per-market choice: A-Book where risk transfer or liquidity matters most, B-Book where the platform prefers to keep the spread or Binance has no market (e.g. YELLOW).

Isolated at the system level, dependent at the business level

An MM failure never blocks our services: trading-api does not wait on the MM (it locks margin and publishes to Kafka; the connector calls the MM asynchronously), and queries, deposits, withdrawals, transfers and B-Book markets keep working.

But on an A-Book market the fill happens at Binance, so we can never assume an outcome. A timed-out order goes UNKNOWN with margin still locked until the MM confirms (3.5); a timed-out cancel is not reported as cancelled; a liquidation close that cannot be sent is real exposure. A long MM outage halts A-Book markets the same way a Finex outage halts B-Book markets. Breakers, UNKNOWN arbitration, reconciliation and liquidation re-issue are launch prerequisites, not options.

Four Preconditions Worth Stating Up Front

Precondition 1: a market switches book only at zero open positions

An A-Book position has a real Binance position behind it; a B-Book position does not. Moving a market between books with positions open would split books from reality, so the first version allows a switch only when that market has no open positions and no resting orders.

The initial launch meets this trivially: trading is halted and treated as a fresh start (confirmed 2026-09-24).

Precondition 2: triggers and liquidation stay local

User TP/SL trigger evaluation, WAL, and LockPerpAsset stay local (the perp_trigger Trigger→Lock→CommandOrderExecute flow is unchanged).

The PM Perp liquidation scanner keeps deciding when a position is liquidated (Binance mark on A-Book markets, MMR tiers). On an A-Book market its close order goes to mm-connector instead of Finex. We do not use Binance stop orders for liquidation: Binance caps open conditional orders per symbol per account (10 at the time of writing), far below one per user on a shared master account.

Precondition 3: both books are permanent

Finex perp, takeover/ADL, and the internal market-making accounts stay — they serve B-Book markets. The book flag is a long-lived per-market setting, not a one-off rollback switch.

Precondition 4: isolated margin is enforced on A-Book markets

A-Book markets reject cross-margin settings server-side (PM Perp + trading-api validation) and the frontend hides the Cross toggle. Hedge and one-way both remain available: the master account holds only the net of all user legs.

Why the Kafka-native Option Was Rejected

The alternative was letting the MM consume/produce our Kafka topics directly (no connector needed). Rejected because:

  1. Security boundary — the Kafka cluster network and credentials would be exposed to the MM's service (in-house, but a separately operated system); consumer-group, rebalance, and lag management responsibility shifts outward with an uncontrolled blast radius.
  2. Coupled evolution — internal event schema changes (e.g. adding a field to EventTradeExecuted) would directly break external consumers; a REST/WS contract lets internal formats evolve independently.
  3. MM implementation cost — the MM team knows Binance-style REST/WS. The interface mirrors Binance semantics (order placement / cancels / execution stream), so their implementation is near zero learning cost.
  4. Governance — the connector is a first-class service on our side: rate limiting, circuit breaking, reconciliation, and audit logs all live with us — observable and operable.

Why role matters

Our lock side pre-charges fees by order type (post_only locks at the maker rate); at settlement we charge by the Binance fill role the MM reports: a resting limit that gets hit → role=maker → maker rate; a crossing order → taker rate. Fully aligned with Finex's maker/taker semantics today — fee_engine needs no changes.

3.5 Idempotency · Dangling Orders · Arbitration

A race window that must be closed (review feedback 2026-09-25)

A GET at 500ms returning "not found" does not mean the order will never execute: the POST can still be in flight in the network or the MM's queue, arriving at 800ms and still unexpired (default validity 3s) — if we unlock margin at that point and the user withdraws, the later-arriving fill has no margin backing. expires_at constrains "when the MM receives it", not "when we may declare it dead".

  1. Every submit carries expires_at (our clock + 2 × RTT + processing headroom, default 3s)A request already expired on arrival must be answered with EXPIRED and must not be executed — the validity window is the time precondition of the death verdict, not a sufficient condition (see item 2).
  2. ack timeout (500ms) → the order enters UNKNOWN; margin stays lockedA timeout means "no ack received", not "the MM never received it". A death verdict requires all three conditions: ① expires_at + clock-skew headroom (default +1s) has passed; ② a GET at that point still finds no record; ③ the MM provides a non-executable terminal guarantee for that client_order_id (tombstone, item 3). Any "not found" before expiry is treated as UNKNOWN with margin locked, shown to the user as pending.
  3. MM-side tombstone guarantee (contract addition)The first time the MM answers a GET with "no record" for a client_order_id it must persist that answer; any later POST with the same ID is rejected. This upgrades "not found now" into "will never execute" — without it, the death verdict does not stand.
  4. The MM's own UNKNOWN obligation (contract addition)When the MM is uncertain about its Binance submission result (MM→Binance timeout), it must not report FILLED / REJECTED — it must keep the order in-flight until certain. The same problem is pushed down one level; the MM is not allowed to close its own window with a guess.
  5. CANCEL_REJECTED(FILLED) must not be ignoredWe may not yet have received all fill reports locally (WS gap): first GET to backfill the fill sequence, settle each fill, and only then may the order go terminal.
  6. WS disconnect → full arbitration after reconnectFirst step on reconnect: GET every in-flight and UNKNOWN client_order_id, aligning states and fill sequences; fill_id dedup guarantees no double settlement.
  7. Double idempotency insurance on settlementThe connector's synthesized events carry IdempotencyKey = client_order_id:fill_id; PM Perp's existing idempotency cache dedups natively (reusing Settlement.HandleTrade's Set/Delete logic).

3.8 Netted Master Account Rules (v1.5)

Every A-Book order from every user lands on one one-way Binance account, which holds only the net of all user legs across both position modes. Example: a single hedge-mode user long 0.5 and short 1 leaves the master account short 0.5. That is correct and needs no special handling, but it makes the following rules mandatory for the MM.

  • Plain buy/sell only — never send reduceOnly or closePosition, and never cancel by symbol. All three act on the account's net position, i.e. on other users' exposure. Reduce-only is enforced on our side (quantity capped at the user's leg).
  • Cancel one order at a time — by client_order_id only; a symbol-wide cancel would cancel every user's orders on that market.
  • Liquidation closes complete in full — a source=liquidation market close that only partially fills keeps executing until the requested quantity is done, each fill reported as usual; if it cannot complete, report the remainder explicitly so we re-issue.
  • Priority — liquidation closes go ahead of user orders in the MM's rate-limit budget.
  • No Binance conditional orders for our users — user TP/SL and liquidation triggers are evaluated locally (perp_trigger / PM Perp scanner). Binance caps open conditional orders per symbol per account (10 at the time of writing) and moved them to the Algo Service (/fapi/v1/algoOrder); neither fits one shared account serving many users.

Fees on netted flow

Both legs of a hedged user really trade on Binance, so the master account pays Binance fees on each leg while its net position barely moves. Our user fee rates on A-Book markets must stay above the master account's Binance rates (maker and taker), or every fill loses money. This is a pricing decision to confirm with the business before launch.

A close that does not come back

If the close is rejected, times out into UNKNOWN, or is cut short, the position stays locked and the scanner re-issues the remaining quantity after arbitration (3.5) — never a second parallel close. A liquidation overdue alert fires when mark has been past the liquidation price for T seconds without a completed close.

Order amendment

Not supported — same as today: cancel and re-place. Liquidation / batch close flow through the same command path; the connector submits one by one.

5 · Settlement & Accounting

The House Account: the Last Piece of Double-Entry

On an A-Book market every user fill is a real trade on our own Binance master account — but our books must record where every payment obligation to users comes from. The solution: one new system account — the house account — as the counterparty leg of every A-Book trade. It is the ledger mirror of the master account, preserving Scheme A double-entry and the audit chain by construction. B-Book markets settle exactly as today.

House Account Definition

Reuses the existing system-account machinery (the spec list in raft_recovery/system_accounts.go + EnsureSystemAccounts idempotent reconciliation; UUID suffix continues at 09). But the house sits on the hot path of every single trade — it cannot be estimated as "just another account"; it must be benchmarked and validated as a core-settlement change (review feedback 2026-09-25):

// portfolio_manager_perp/perpetuals/domain/models/const.go addition:
MMHouseAccountAppSessionID = "00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000009"
MMHouseAccountID          = "00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000009"
// owner = SystemAccountOwner (0x0), PrincipalKindService, auto-provisioned via EnsureSystemAccounts
  • Semantics — house = the ledger mirror of our Binance master account. Its realized PnL equals the master account's trading PnL on user flow; its position per market is the negative of the users' aggregate. There is no third party to settle with: house balance is reconciled against the master account's wallet, not paid out.
  • Master account margin is a platform risk — isolated margin exists only in our books. On Binance the whole net position shares one account margin, so the master account itself can be liquidated. Its margin ratio is monitored and breakered (page 8); the Binance leverage/margin setup of the master account is an operations decision, not derived from users.
  • Hot spot & concurrency — every USDT-quoted market shares one house and every trade writes it: Raft apply throughput and the dbWriter's perps_balances hot-row upsert must be measured. Fallback: per-market house pools following the insurance-fund Pool 1/2/3 precedent (1:1 with Binance symbols, reconciliation compares per market, aggregation is a plain sum).
  • Bookkeeping form: two-way HEDGE legs — every trade atomically books the user leg and the house leg (same journal), keeping both sides' realized PnL strictly in sync — no timing skew with many users sharing the account. A net-position variant would require changing open/close classification (IsClosingOrder) semantics and breaks this sync; rejected. House legs carry zero allocated margin and zero fees.
  • Risk exemptions (explicitly implemented, not obtained by default) — negative balances allowed, no MMR, excluded from liquidation scanning; synthetic orders bypass normal order validation; PrincipalKindService excludes it from user statistics / rankings / rewards / analytics aggregation, per the insurance-fund precedent.
  • No-double-booking invariant — on liquidation, a user's loss is booked exactly once in trade settlement; the bankruptcy shortfall is expressed only via the insurance → house transfer; the two must never stack. Asserted in test/v2 integration scenarios.

The economic loop (a user long, start to finish — v1.4, own master account)

User opens long 10 TAO @199 (isolated) → we lock the isolated margin → the MM buys 10 TAO on our master account → house books the short leg against the user → the scanner starts watching the position's liquidation price.

User closes the long @205 → user +60 (realized PnL credited), house −60; on Binance the master account sold the 10 TAO for +60. The cash backing the user's gain is already in our own account — nothing to collect from anyone.

When users lose, the direction reverses: house gains, and the master account's Binance loss on that flow equals it. At any instant house mirrors the master account's user-flow position and PnL.

When two users are natural counterparties (A buys, B sells) both orders are still forwarded to Binance as-is; they simply net inside the master account's position. We never internalize matching on an A-Book market. The same holds for one hedge-mode user holding both legs: both legs trade on Binance, the account nets them, and both pay fees (3.8).

Funding Pass-Through

  • Rate & interval — Binance values used directly: the mark oracle's funding field already has a Binance source; perps_markets.funding_interval_seconds already supports variable intervals (the rollout seeded 4h/8h per Binance fundingInfo). PM Perp's funding worker and funding_payments settlement logic unchanged.
  • Accounting path — user funding paid/received books against the house account. Binance charges the master account on its net position while we charge each user on their own position; with users on both sides these differ (longs and shorts net on Binance but each pays or receives with us). The difference is house PnL, booked explicitly per funding event and reconciled against the master account's Binance funding records.
  • Liquidation prices move with funding — each funding settlement changes isolated margins; the scanner simply evaluates against the updated margins on its next pass. Nothing is sent to the MM, so there is no burst of external calls at the funding timestamp.
  • Cap / interest — in pass-through mode the cap always equals the actual Binance value; our self-set caps become unnecessary (current table values already mirror Binance, no change needed).

Liquidation & the Insurance Fund

  1. Scanner unchanged — trigger evaluation, MMR tiers, and warning bands are retained; on A-Book markets it evaluates against Binance mark.
  2. Execution on Binance — the close is a source=liquidation market order through the MM; the reported fills settle as ExecType=liquidation at Binance's real fill price (Scheme A journal_id grouping reused). That fill price is the liquidation price the user sees in their history.
  3. Settlement order — realized PnL → liquidation fee → remaining isolated margin returned to the user's available balance → cancel the user's other orders on that market (page 4).
  4. Bankruptcy handling — the part of a user's loss exceeding their isolated margin: insurance fund → house account transfer (the existing CLEARING_MIRROR SettleLiquidatedPosition semantics retained, simplified from "settlement pool takes over the position" to "pay the difference directly"). Isolation caps the user's loss at that one position's margin.
  5. Not used on A-Book — takeover (insurance fund inheriting positions) and ADL (auto-deleveraging counterparties) stay for B-Book markets but are never used on A-Book markets, which have no internal counterparties to protect. There the insurance fund only covers bankruptcy shortfalls.

Implementation notes for the depth mirror (incl. diff semantics, review feedback 2026-09-25)

Binance depth20 is a snapshot stream (no diff-alignment problem) — full 20 levels every 100ms → maps directly onto the existing OrderBookSnapshot semantics; increments are synthesized by quote as snapshot diffs, with two hard rules: vanishing levels must emit a qty→0 deletion increment; after reconnect, first re-publish a full snapshot to rebuild the diff baseline (no giant diff across the outage window). The current client mix of increment/snapshot consumption is preserved.

Note: the mirrored book contains only Binance's orders — our users' resting orders are not on it. A user's own order state arrives via the private stream (EventOrderUpdated); the frontend overlays "my orders" on the mirrored book (frontend change item, tracked in the cutover plan).

ledger: External Kline Ingestion Design (not "add a consumer and write directly")

Review feedback (2026-09-25): today's ledger generates continuous aggregates from trades, and higher timeframes build on those; external 1m klines cannot slot into the existing structure merely by adding a consumer that writes directly — a new storage and aggregation design is required.

  1. New source table + UPSERT semantics — a new external-kline source table (symbol, bucket, OHLCV, event-time watermark): Binance kline events carry the cumulative volume of the running minute, so an unclosed bar is overwritten event by event within its minute; duplicates / out-of-order events are idempotently upserted by event-time watermark (a late old value never rewinds an updated bucket).
  2. Disconnect backfill — WS outage windows are backfilled via REST /fapi/v1/klines through the same upsert path, naturally idempotent; when backfill and the live stream race, the higher watermark wins.
  3. Aggregation chain rebuild — perp markets' higher-timeframe CAGGs are re-pointed from the trades source to the new source table; spot and transition-period markets keep the trades chain — two chains coexist, routed per market, not a global switch.
  4. History continuity — klines aggregated from internal trades are retained; the switch date is the boundary; frontend charts stitch across it (continuous time axis, no rewrite).
  5. User trade history — unaffected: user trade records have always lived in PM Perp (perps_transactions / position history), independent of public klines.

Mark Price & Risk Data

  • Mark source per book — A-Book markets use Binance mark directly (OKX/Bybit remain only as a staleness / divergence alarm), because the position really lives on Binance and liquidation must track the price its hedge is exposed to. B-Book markets keep today's multi-source oracle. The min_sources = 1 rule never changes (the rollout runbook's permanent-lock clause stays in force).
  • InternalPerpTradeCollector disabled — the "internal trade self-price" source goes offline for A-Book markets (user fills are sparse and priced at Binance anyway — no information content).
  • Liquidation reference price — on A-Book markets the mark shown in the UI, the one the scanner evaluates, and the one Binance applies to the master account are the same number.
  • exchangeInfo changes — when Binance tightens filters, quote syncs within the hour; resting orders that become invalid are cleaned up naturally by MM-side INVALID_FILTER reports.

The boundary of suspended liquidation

While an A-Book market is disconnected, user margin ratios keep deteriorating (mark never stops); recovery closes at worse prices — the insurance-fund share grows, but no orphaned positions can appear (our books stay self-consistent).

That is the cost of the "degrade, never lose funds" principle; it must be included in insurance-fund capacity modeling (open items). B-Book markets never depend on the MM.

A-Book fee schedule before first market

User maker/taker rates on A-Book markets must stay above the master account's Binance rates, including both legs of hedged users (3.8). Also decide the liquidation fee rate and whether isolated positions need per-position margin warnings (today's 80/90/95 bands are cross-only).

First A-Book markets before phase 4

Pick the first 2–3 markets. Criteria depend on why A-Book is wanted: risk transfer (largest one-sided exposure), liquidity (thinnest internal book with real volume), or both — to confirm with the business.

Spot A-Book phase 2

Three blockers to resolve before designing it: ① asset mismatch — we custody on-chain WBTC / WSOL while Binance spot trades BTC / SOL, so a user buying WBTC with us is hedged in a different asset; ② treasury rebalancing — net user buying accumulates BTC and drains USDT on Binance while withdrawals come from the Cage vault, which needs a finance-run rebalancing process between Binance and the vault; ③ unlisted assets — YELLOWUSDT has no Binance market and stays B-Book; spot PM also has no house / system-account machinery yet.

Market-order slippage protection before first market

Binance has no native slippage parameter. Phase 1 ships the lock-side buffer factor; a later phase may add an API-level max_slippage (needs MM cooperation via a dedicated error code, or our post-hoc arbitration).

Emergency liquidation capacity during MM disconnect before first market

Bankruptcy expansion while A-Book liquidation is suspended is absorbed by the insurance fund. Stress-test basis: 30 minutes of disconnect + ±20% market move, plus the master-account liquidation scenario (page 8).

Compliance & account attribution blocks first production market

The platform's own Binance account entity, regional compliance, and the regulatory characterization of "user orders executed on the platform's own exchange account" — needs the compliance team's opinion; does not block engineering.
