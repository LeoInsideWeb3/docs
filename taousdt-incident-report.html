1 · Verdict

This was a hack, not customer withdrawals

At least 15 coordinated attacker accounts exploited a flaw in the TAOUSDT-PERP liquidation / take-over mechanism to create profit out of thin air, converted it to YELLOW tokens, and withdrew it on-chain overnight. The drop in the custody-contract balance that customer service noticed is the on-chain footprint of that theft.

Confirmed theft — funds are still moving

~$251,020 USDT was extracted from the perpetuals system into attacker accounts. Part of it (~424k YELLOW + ~17k USDT + small ETH/WBTC) has already left the platform on-chain. A further 221,831 YELLOW is still sitting in an attacker account, available to withdraw at any moment — withdrawal processing must stay paused and that account must stay frozen.

2 · What Happened

The alert, in plain words

No prior knowledge of the trading system is needed for this page. It explains what "platform balance on the smart contract" means and what actually moved overnight.

The alert

  • When — overnight, 2026-09-17 → 2026-09-18 (all times in this report are UTC)
  • Who raised it — customer service: "platform balance on smart contract went down during this night a lot"
  • Which contract — the custody vault 0x3a9624f9BB2Dc43E90c0699DDc4A9b6BEc147Cde — the on-chain safe that holds customer assets backing platform balances

Two very different stories that fit the same symptom

Story Ainnocent — large customer withdrawal

A real user who deposited and traded for a long time asks to withdraw a large balance. The vault pays out, the balance drops. Everything the platform's books say matches the chain. Nobody lost anything.

Story Bcriminal — funds manufactured then drained

Someone tricks the trading system into creating balances that were never earned or deposited, converts them to tokens, and withdraws. The vault pays out exactly the same way — the difference is that the money being paid out was never real. The loss lands on the platform (insurance pool), not on any customer.

How we tell them apart

One accounting identity decides it: every legitimate withdrawal has a matching record of where the money came from — a deposit, a trade, a transfer, a reward. We traced every large withdrawal of the night back through the platform's ledgers.

What we found

Every big withdrawal of the night traced back to the same dead end: a perpetual-futures account opened minutes earlier, funded with ~$1,100, that "earned" $23,400–$24,082 within minutes — in identical amounts across unrelated accounts — then handed the money to a fresh spot account that bought YELLOW and withdrew. That is Story B, executed by a coordinated group.

Money does not behave like this by accident

5 · Root Cause

How the money was manufactured

The flaw is now confirmed in code and data: a position whose allocated margin was drained to zero/negative while still open produced an absurd bankruptcy price (0 or half of market), and the liquidation settlement paid the attacker's short as if it had closed at that price.

Background you need (30 seconds)

  • Perpetuals ("perps") — futures-style contracts with leverage; profit and loss settle in USDT
  • Liquidation / take-over — when a leveraged trader cannot cover their losses, the system seizes and closes the position; the system itself becomes the counterparty (it appears in trade records as the zero address 0x000…000); normally the seized trader is closed at a penalty price and the difference is covered by the insurance pool
  • Custody vault — the on-chain safe (the contract from the CS report); on-chain withdrawals are paid from it

The flaw, confirmed (v3 — data-verified)

100x cross shorts → stress-equity liquidation → junk bankruptcy price → pool pays the pre-positioned book

Five facts, each verified in evidence E7/E10/E11/E12:

  1. All exploit take-overs are CROSS margin (12 of 14 records) — the cross bankruptcy formula is bankr = entry + account_equity / size where equity = account balance + worst-case stress uPnL of the position (calculator/cross.go:206).
  2. The mule accounts never transferred anything out — their perps ledgers show spot→perps IN transfers only (E12). The earlier "margin drained via transfer" hypothesis is refuted; the transfer check was never even invoked.
  3. The shorts were deliberately thin — mules deposited ~$1,000–4,550 and opened 100x shorts of up to 123 TAO (~$28k notional). At 100x, any adverse stress computation drives equity deeply negative on its own: the recorded bankruptcy prices reverse-compute account equity of −$14,890 (123-TAO record) and ≈ −entry×size for the bankr=0 probes.
  4. Bankruptcy price = attacker-controlled — by choosing balance-vs-notional, the mules landed bankr at 0.000 (11 records) or 108.73 (half of market). The settlement closed their shorts at those prices (the zero-address trades) — a short settling at ~0 books near-full notional as "profit"-side settlement.
  5. The payout side was pre-positioned — the whales were the maker side of TAOUSDT-PERP: 860–1,382 TAO of maker volume each at 230.5→284, placed 1–2 minutes before each take-over. The pool's stage-2 disposal (IOC at mark, sweeping the book) bought into those orders: ~860 TAO × ~$28/unit = the deterministic ~$24,082.87 per whale.

The exact per-position arithmetic (stress price source, fee/funding components) needs the Raft logs — the structural chain above is fully confirmed by database records.

The chain, step by step (plain language)

  1. Find the flawProbe accounts sell tiny amounts while deliberately triggering the take-over path. Some probes execute at price 0 — trades that a healthy system would never match. The system counterparty accepts them, which reveals the flaw: the take-over settlement can be made to pay out at attacker-chosen prices.
  2. Force the system to buyThe exploiters sell ~910 TAO (~$210k notional) onto the system counterparty at market price. The system's side of these trades is funded from the insurance pool — real platform money leaves the system here.
  3. Collect as "trading profit"The perp accounts of the freshly-opened whale accounts register large gains and transfer them to their spot accounts — 12 transfers, $251,020.94. The identical amounts (24,082.87 ×4, 23,400.00 ×2 …) show a fixed formula, not market outcomes.
  4. Convert to YELLOWThe loot buys YELLOW at ~0.0356 USDT — a price made favourable by a separate cluster churning the YELLOW market an hour earlier. Converting to YELLOW moves the value into a token the vault pays out on-chain.
  5. Drain the vault109 withdrawals of 6,000–8,888 YELLOW each (sized to stay under per-transaction limits), 52 small ETH withdrawals, 14 USDT withdrawals. This is the balance drop customer service saw.

Important nuance for engineers

The withdrawal system worked correctly — every withdrawal was properly reserved, debited, and finalized; withdrawals-vs-ledger reconciliation matches.

The theft happened one layer earlier: the liquidation / take-over settlement paid out money it should never have paid, and those balances were then withdrawn quite legitimately.

This is why withdrawal monitoring alone cannot catch this class of attack — the take-over settlement path is the control that failed.

6 · Send Network

The YELLOW send machine (restricted feature)

Response to the CS question: "identify the exact list of accounts doing spot_sends — this functionality is restricted for regular users and needs specific configuration." The answer uncovered a second, larger machine running since 09-15.

How the restriction works (code)

  • Spot sends (SendSpotFunds, portfolio_manager/spot/spot_service.go:4271) is not exposed on the regular user API surface — it ships with the agent-subaccount feature (provisioning a subaccount yields account_kind = agent, spot/provision.go:16)
  • The ledger layer only checks ownership: sender account's owner must match the caller. It does not check account_kind — so an owner with an agent subaccount can send from any of their accounts, including old "normal" ones
  • 13 owners obtained agent subaccounts between 2026-09-10 and 09-16 — days before the exploit. Several of those owners are the main exploiters (0x535c…, 0xe1ae…) and hold large old accounts from March
